Version: 1.0 · Prepared: 11 October 2026

## Purpose and authority

Use this reference to generate creatures, lineages, characters, scenes, societies, and progression histories that obey the same world rules. This is a bounded reconstruction, not a claim to have recovered the entire original conversation.

**Confirmed** means explicitly required by the author in the current request or stated in a retrieved user decision. **Provisional** means a proposal awaiting adoption. **Unresolved** means the available evidence does not settle the mechanic. Generation safeguards explain how to apply canon without adding hidden mechanics; they are not additional laws of the world.

Creative names, characters, places, and examples are drafts until the author accepts them. A plausible explanation, repeated example, or assistant suggestion does not confirm a rule. An explicit author revision can change canon; record what it replaces and review affected material.

## Confirmed rules

### C01 — Energy is conserved

Energy cannot appear from nowhere. Changes in stored energy, growth, reproduction, evolution, or enhancement must be supported by energy already present or an identified input. Transfers debit their source. Energy described as lost leaves the usable pool; it is not deleted from the total accounting. Its exact destination may remain unresolved. [S0, S5]

Conservation does not establish the world's energy sources, absorption rules, conversion efficiencies, or measurement units. Do not introduce kill rewards, ambient absorption, or automatic replenishment as canon.

### C02 — Levels belong to the current species/form

A level describes progression within a particular species/form. It is not a universal measure that can be compared across all creatures without context. Evolution into a chosen new form resets that form's level progression. [S0]

Record the form alongside the level. The reset's starting number, caps, thresholds, and cross-form strength comparisons are unresolved. A level reset does not itself establish that memories, skills, age, or all physical strength reset.

### C03 — Evolution involves choice, including timing

Evolution branches are choices. A creature can deliberately delay evolution to acquire further experience rather than selecting immediately. Reaching a cap does not force an immediate transformation. [S1–S3]

The available evidence does not establish every prerequisite for evolution, how choice works for creatures of different intelligence, or whether evolution can fail.

### C04 — Delaying evolution at the level cap is stable

Remaining in the capped form is stable. Delay alone does not impose an invented collapse, compulsory transformation, or instability countdown. [S3]

Stability does not establish immortality, freedom from hunger, unlimited energy storage, or retention of every evolution option. General needs and hazards remain subject to whatever rules are actually established.

### C05 — Evolution must be biologically compatible

Available transformations must be compatible with the creature's biology. Experiences influence which compatible possibilities are available; experience does not justify arbitrary transformation into any desired creature. [S0]

Compatibility criteria, transformation limits, and the relation between species and forms remain unfinished. Where the reference cannot establish a specific branch's compatibility, label the branch conditional or proposed.

### C06 — Evolution options change with experience and time

Experience and environment can change the currently available choices. An Ice Dragon that travels to a volcano can have different choices from an Ice Dragon living in frozen mountains. Some choices can disappear if the creature waits too long. [S0, S2]

This does not confirm a particular volcanic form, a guaranteed fire adaptation, a required exposure duration, or a permanent unlock. Exact acquisition, loss, and reacquisition conditions are unresolved.

### C07 — Visibility concerns current evolution options

Evolution-option visibility is current: present choices are the operative visible set, not an established catalogue of all future branches. [S3]

How this information is perceived, whether there is a system interface, and whether a creature remembers previously visible choices are unresolved. Do not turn this rule into a confirmed HUD, prophecy, or complete hidden evolution tree.

### C08 — Evolution consumes all evolution reserve

When evolution occurs, all energy in the evolution reserve is consumed by that event. It cannot also remain available afterward or fund another action without a new input. [S4]

“All” refers to the evolution reserve, not automatically every energy store in the body. The mechanism can transform or expend that energy; conservation still applies. Consumption does not establish perfect conversion efficiency, unlimited benefits, or a minimum reserve required for success.

### C09 — Evolution-reserve capacity is biological

The creature's biology determines the capacity of its evolution reserve. Do not assume unlimited storage or a universal capacity shared by every form. [S4]

Exact capacities, overflow behavior, refilling, changes at evolution, and the relationship between this reserve and next-level energy are unresolved.

### C10 — Evolved forms can be inherited

Offspring can naturally be born in an evolved form. A naturally born evolved species follows the same progression rules rather than receiving a separate privileged system. [S0, S1]

Birth form is distinct from the parent's attained level. Do not automatically copy parental level, reserve, skills, or experiences to an offspring. Exact inheritance outcomes and newborn levels remain unresolved.

### C11 — Compatible evolved forms can produce hybrids

Reproduction between biologically compatible evolved forms can produce hybrids, including different evolved dragon forms. [S0, S1]

Hybrid traits, fertility, viability, birth form, and evolution branches require compatible biology; do not automatically combine every parental advantage. The reference does not establish probabilities or a universal crossbreeding rule.

### C12 — Preserve the illustrative dragon lineage

Dragon → Ice Dragon → Arctic Dragon is the established illustrative evolution lineage. [S1]

It illustrates successive forms, each with its own progression and a level reset on chosen evolution. It is not established as the only dragon lineage or a compulsory route. An inherited Ice Dragon need not first live as a Dragon merely to satisfy this example.

### C13 — Reproduction uses some next-level energy, with loss

Some energy accumulated toward the next levels goes into reproduction, and some is lost from the usable pool. Both portions must be deducted from the source energy; neither can simultaneously remain available for progression. [S5]

Preserve this unfinished reproduction rule when depicting offspring formation. Exact timing within formation, amounts, recipient stores, and later development costs remain unresolved. Do not claim all reproduction costs occur at one instant or invent a fixed split. The relationship between this energy and the evolution reserve is not yet settled.

### C14 — Skills and energy enhancement are distinct

Do not equate a skill with an increase in energy or an energy-funded enhancement. Track what the creature can do separately from any enhancement that improves its performance. [S0]

The skill system has only begun to be defined. Acquisition, practice, ranks, retention across evolution, inheritance, activation costs, and the exact nature and permanence of energy enhancement are unresolved. A technique may involve energy in a proposed implementation, but that does not erase the distinction or confirm the implementation.

## Generation safeguards and consistency checks

These are bookkeeping and review methods, not additional canon.

1. **Label status.** Identify confirmed constraints, draft setting details, provisional mechanics, and unresolved dependencies. Cite C-IDs when reviewing a mechanical claim.
2. **Track continuity.** Keep current form and level separate from earlier forms, inherited traits, experiences, skills, enhancements, and energy stores. Use “unspecified” rather than fabricated numbers.
3. **Use one energy account.** An abstract audit can use: opening energy + identified inputs = closing energy + energy transferred into other tracked stores + energy expended or leaving the usable pool. Define the boundary so transferred energy is not counted twice. If offspring are inside the boundary, their received energy is stored within it, not also an external outflow. This is accounting notation, not a progression formula.
4. **Do not duplicate stores.** Until their relationship is decided, do not count the same next-level energy and evolution reserve as two independent balances. Any reproduction/evolution interaction involving both is conditional.
5. **Handle the cap honestly.** Delay can create opportunities for experience. It does not authorize cap-breaking levels, endless reserve growth, free offspring, or unchanged options. Whether delay gives an additional mechanical advantage remains open.
6. **Keep unknowns local.** Generate the portions supported by canon. Leave necessary unknown values unspecified, or offer clearly labeled alternatives. Ask only when an unresolved rule determines the requested result.
7. **Avoid false validation.** A qualitative energy account can be consistent without proving a quantitative balance. An outcome depending on an unknown cost or compatibility rule is conditional, not fully verified.
