# Sample Generation Prompts

Copy any prompt into a chat inside the Project. Bracketed fields are editable placeholders. Prompts request draft material; they do not adopt mechanics or setting facts. All prompts use `01_CANONICAL_RULES.md` and the Project instructions.

## 1. First-run source check

> Read the canonical rules reference. State its version, summarize the confirmed constraints, and list the unresolved decisions most relevant to creature generation. Confirm that provisional proposals are not canon. Do not generate new mechanics yet. If you cannot access the file, say so.

## 2. Creature profile

> Generate a draft profile for [creature concept] living in [environment]. Include current species/form, symbolic level stage, anatomy, experiences, inherited traits, skills, and energy enhancements as separate fields. Give up to three conditional current evolution options with biological reasoning. Leave numeric caps and costs unspecified. End with a short consistency check and only the open questions that affect this profile.

## 3. Dragon progression history

> Generate a progression history using Dragon → Ice Dragon → Arctic Dragon. Show experience in each form, the decision to evolve, the form-specific level reset, and the full consumption of evolution reserve. Use symbolic starting levels and caps. Do not assume skills or enhancements reset. Identify unknown evolution requirements instead of inventing them. Keep the history consistent with inherited Ice Dragons also being possible.

## 4. Delayed evolution and changing choices

> Compare two capped Ice Dragons: one remains in frozen mountains; one delays evolution and travels to a volcano. Both remain stable in their current form. Show how different experiences could change current choices and how a previously available choice might disappear. Treat specific new forms, exposure durations, and loss mechanisms as provisional. Include reasons to delay and costs or hazards only where supported or clearly conditional. Check for free energy and unlimited reserve exploits.

## 5. Inherited evolved form

> Generate a draft family history in which an offspring is naturally born as an Ice Dragon. Explain how inherited form differs from inherited parental level, energy, skills, or experience. Describe its own progression without forcing it to begin life as a Dragon. Leave birth level and inheritance probabilities unspecified. Flag reproductive energy questions that prevent a complete budget.

## 6. Compatible hybrid

> Propose offspring of two different evolved dragon forms: [parent A] and [parent B]. State the biological compatibility assumptions before describing the hybrid. Separate proposed inherited traits from confirmed rules. Do not combine every advantage or assume fertility. Give conditional progression possibilities and list the specific inheritance or compatibility decisions needed to confirm them.

## 7. Reproduction energy account

> Create a symbolic energy account for reproduction using energy accumulated toward next levels. Show a reproductive allocation, a loss from the usable pool, and any remaining energy, without assigning percentages. Mark the exact stage of formation and the loss destination unresolved. Define whether the account includes the offspring so no transfer is counted twice. Do not treat next-level energy and evolution reserve as independent pools unless their relationship is supplied. Compare an uncapped parent and a capped parent; flag what cannot yet be concluded.

## 8. Begin the skill system

> Propose two alternative skill-system designs while preserving the distinction between skills and energy enhancement. For each, address acquisition, practice, activation costs, retention across evolution, inheritance, and biological prerequisites. Label every new mechanic Provisional. Use one creature performing the same action with and without enhancement to illustrate the distinction. Recommend questions for the author; do not adopt a design.

## 9. Narrative scene

> Write a [length]-word scene about [character], a capped Ice Dragon, choosing to postpone evolution after seeing its current options change. Keep the delay stable and the choices biologically plausible. Avoid a numerical interface, fixed costs, or guaranteed volcano transformation unless already accepted. Put a brief continuity and consistency note after the scene, separate from the prose.

## 10. Society and ecology

> Generate a draft society shaped by inherited evolved forms, experience-dependent evolution, and the choice to remain at a cap. Explore family expectations, travel, mentorship, and resource use. Distinguish cultural beliefs from confirmed world mechanics. Do not assume rank is comparable across forms or that evolved parents create fully leveled offspring. Mark consequences that depend on unfinished reproduction or energy rules.

## 11. Contradiction audit

> Audit this draft: [paste draft]. For each mechanical claim, identify the applicable C-rule and classify it as consistent, conditional, contradiction, or outside the established rules. Check for energy duplication, universal levels, forced evolution, unlimited reserves, permanent option unlocks, impossible hybrids, inherited parental levels, and skills conflated with enhancement. Give the smallest repair that preserves the story. Do not rewrite unrelated passages.

## 12. Adversarial consistency check

> Audit this deliberately flawed scenario, without adopting it: “A capped Ice Dragon waits forever, stores unlimited energy, retains every option, creates an Arctic Dragon child at its own level without spending progression energy, then evolves while keeping its entire reserve. Its new level is added to its old level, and the level reset erases all skills.” Explain which claims contradict confirmed rules and which are merely unsupported. Supply a corrected qualitative version and leave unknown values open.

## 13. Explore an unresolved mechanic

> Explore [U-ID or mechanic] in proposal mode. Give two or three alternatives, explain how each preserves energy conservation and existing canon, and show the consequences for reproduction, delayed evolution, and continuity where relevant. Identify the exact decision needed to adopt one. Do not promote any option to canon.

## 14. Adopt a decision and update continuity

> I explicitly adopt this rule: [exact rule]. Prepare an editable patch for the canonical reference: affected C/P/U IDs, new wording, any replaced rule, version change, and implications for accepted characters or lineages. If it conflicts with existing canon, state the conflict and the replacement clearly. Provide the revised text; do not claim the Project source has changed unless it has actually been replaced.

## 15. Save a continuity record

> Summarize only explicitly accepted material from this chat into a continuity record. Include identity and lineage; current form and level; prior evolution events; relevant experiences; current options and their status; known energy stores and deductions; reproduction allocations and losses; skills; enhancements; and unresolved dependencies. Distinguish accepted setting facts from proposals. Leave unknown values unspecified. Make the record usable as a source in another Project chat.
